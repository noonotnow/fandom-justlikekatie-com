| --- | --- | --- |
| `wei fei yuan mir`, clarified as “ashes fly … extinguished” | 灰飞烟灭 — **huī fēi yān miè** | Ashes scatter and smoke vanishes; figuratively, utterly destroyed or completely gone. A strong identification from the creator's meaning clue; dictionary spelling and meaning verified. |
| `lu shi shei dan wei kezhi`, clarified as “who gets the deer no one can know yet” | 鹿死谁手 — **lù sǐ shéi shǒu**; likely full line 鹿死谁手，尚未可知 — **lù sǐ shéi shǒu, shàng wèi kě zhī** | The creator's deer explanation identifies the core idiom: literally “in whose hands will the deer die?”, meaning who wins or takes the prize. The full line means “It is still too early to say who will win.” Its exact spoken ending remains a reconstruction, not a verified quotation from a particular drama. 谁 also has the dictionary pronunciation **shuí**. |
| `wu hui`, confirmed by the creator as “misunderstanding” | 误会 — **wùhuì** | Misunderstanding; the creator explicitly identifies the intended phrase and its recurring C-drama use. This is not 无悔 (“no regrets”). |

灰飞烟灭 does not literally contain “soul”: 灰 is ash, 烟 is smoke, and 灭
means extinguish or perish. A xianxia subtitle's treatment of spiritual
destruction needs its own context; do not infer a universal reincarnation rule
from the idiom alone.

## Family A: Chinese phrase games

The proposed emphasis is recognizable genre dialogue: what a line means,
what it is doing in the scene, and why its dramatic register is memorable.
These can be useful and playful without turning the whole experience into a
general-purpose Mandarin course.

| Working title | Type | What the player does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What Did They Just Say? | Phrase recognition | Match a reviewed Chinese phrase to its meaning and function in a short scene; distinguish literal meaning from intent. |
| Finish the Dramatic Line | Tile / sentence-building puzzle | Arrange reviewed phrase chunks to complete a recognizable line, with optional pinyin support. |
| Literal or Legendary? | Idiom interpretation | Match a genre expression to its actual meaning rather than an amusing literal reading. |
| Say It Like a Wuxia Character | Register / transcreation game | Match an everyday intention to a reviewed dramatic Chinese rendering, then compare why alternatives change the tone. |
| Hear It, Find It | Listening game | Match original, rights-cleared spoken phrases to reviewed characters, pinyin, or meanings; replay without time pressure. |
| The Subtitle Is Technically Correct | Bilingual nuance game | Compare subtitle options and choose which preserves a line's intended meaning, register, or joke in the supplied context. |

### Language integrity before playable content

Start with a small reviewed phrase bank, not an automatically generated list
of supposedly authentic lines. Each entry needs:

- Confirmed Chinese characters, tone-marked pinyin, and accepted variants.
- A literal gloss and a natural contextual meaning, kept distinct.
- A short explanation of register and use: for example, whether a line is
  defensive, threatening, affectionate, formal, or comic in that context.
- The source or clearly labeled original example, and any spoiler boundary.
- Native-speaker review appropriate to the intended genre and audience.
- Audio provenance and permission if the game uses sound.

Where two answers are defensible, accept them or change the question. Do not
mark a player wrong for a legitimate variant. Do not imply that one dramatic
register is universal across xianxia, wuxia, historical drama, and everyday
Mandarin.

The creator's existing native-speaker feedback request on Chinese transcreation
is relevant context. It does not itself certify any particular phrase or game
answer as reviewed.

## Family B: Games that accompany a watch-along

These should work alongside what viewers are actually watching, rather than
require them to stop for a separate long story. They can initially be local,
shared-device, or printable activities; recording this direction does not
require live multiplayer infrastructure or playback synchronization.

| Working title | Type | What the players do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Call the Next Beat | Prediction game | Pause before a reveal, commit to a prediction, then compare it with what the episode actually shows. |
| Secret Trope Missions | Hidden-objective watch game | Each viewer gets a private trope mission, quietly spots occurrences, and reveals their receipts at the end. |
| Character Fantasy Draft | Draft-and-score companion | Pick characters or roles and earn agreed points for on-screen actions, using a shared rule sheet rather than money or betting. |
| Subtext Showdown | Interpretation / party game | Compare “what they said” with “what they meant,” then discuss or vote on the most convincing reading. |
| The Episode Receipts Challenge | Observation game | Notice details supporting a claim, record a local note or timestamp, and compare evidence after the episode. |

### Watch-along boundaries

- Do not pre-answer predictions using future-episode knowledge.
- Use broad, spoiler-light prompts by default. Episode-specific packs require
  a verified boundary; “we are watching this now” is not permission to reveal
  later material.
- Distinguish an interpretation vote from an objectively correct answer.
- Keep the activity playable without uploading episode footage, transcripts,
  private viewing notes, or individual watch histories.
- Never infer that a group game requires accounts, hosted rooms, synchronized
  video, chat moderation, or persistent player tracking by default.

## Family C: Creative fandom games

These are about making something together, not only recognizing an answer.

| Working title | Type | What the players create |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trope Remix | Prompt-card / writing game | Draw a trope, a constraint, and an inconvenient prop; make a tiny scene that uses all three, then compare interpretations. |
| Write the Missing Subtitle | Caption game | Write an alternate subtitle for an original or authorized scene prompt; compare the funniest, sharpest, or most plausible versions. |
| One Line, Three Intentions | Performance / guessing game | Deliver the same original line as a warning, confession, or farewell; others guess the intended scene. |
| The Trailer Lies | Editing / sequencing game | Arrange the same original scene cards into two trailers that imply very different stories. |

If these later use real stills, clips, or audio, resolve rights and spoiler
boundaries first. Original prompts and art are valid starting material.
An AI judge, voice imitation, public submission feed, and automatic social
posting are not prerequisites.
