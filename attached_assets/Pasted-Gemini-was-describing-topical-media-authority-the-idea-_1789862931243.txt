Gemini was describing **topical/media authority**: the idea that a large, well-organized historical collection can make search engines recognize Fandom Vibes as a useful destination for C-drama actor imagery and fandom context.

It was **not—or should not be—“publish every raw image-search result.”**

## The intended authority loop

Every daily grid creates a durable record containing:

- Actor identity in Chinese, English, and Pinyin
- Assigned vibe and related vocabulary
- Publication date
- Curated 3×3 visual composite
- Original editorial framing
- Related actor, drama, character, and vibe pages
- Source attribution where appropriate
- A stable canonical URL

Over time, that becomes a structured media corpus:

```
Actor page
├── Daily edition A
├── Daily edition B
├── Vibe page
├── Related drama/character
└── Curated visual samples
```

Search engines then see:

- **Depth:** many useful pages about the same domain
- **Consistency:** recurring publication rather than one abandoned actor page
- **Entity clarity:** clear relationships among actors, dramas, characters, and vibes
- **Freshness:** new editions continue appearing
- **Internal authority:** pages link to related pages in an understandable hierarchy
- **Visual relevance:** descriptive images and composites can surface in image search
- **User signals:** people visit, save, share, and return

That is probably what Gemini meant by becoming a **media authority**.

## Where “engine results” fit

The search engines feeding Vibe Atlas—Baidu, Brave, and others—give you a large pool of candidate media. That pool can improve the product because it lets Vibe Atlas:

- curate stronger historical grids;
- identify recurring visual themes;
- classify official stills, event photos, posters, and fan edits;
- develop richer actor/vibe taxonomies;
- preserve provenance;
- generate distinctive editorial collections.

But the raw engine results should normally remain **retrieval infrastructure**, not crawlable pages.

### Public and indexable

- `/actor/liu-xueyi`
- `/vibe/xianmen-lengyu`
- `/vibe-atlas/archive/2026-09-19`
- An original editorial description
- A standard-resolution curated composite
- Approved sample images
- Related editions and actors
- Appropriate source credits

### Protected or private

- Raw search-result JSON
- Full candidate-image inventories
- Search spells and ranking diagnostics
- High-resolution source URLs
- Duplicate and rejected results
- Unpublished actor/vibe combinations
- Internal confidence scores
- Complete paid actor-pack payloads

## Why publishing raw results would backfire

A huge set of automatically generated image-result pages could look like:

- thin or duplicate content;
- scraped search pages;
- doorway pages created only to catch queries;
- copyright-sensitive republishing;
- low-quality URLs that waste crawler attention.

That can weaken the site rather than establish authority.

The authority comes from **Katie’s transformation of the results**:

> retrieval → judgment → classification → editorial context → durable artifact
> 

Not from owning the largest pile of URLs found by Baidu. That pile is infrastructure. The curated historical grid is the publication.

## How this works with the paywall

A historical edition can simultaneously be:

1. **A crawlable media page**
    - Actor, vibe, date, description, composite preview, related content
2. **A Collector product preview**
    - “This archive contains the complete nine-card edition”
3. **A protected interactive product**
    - Full board, individual cards, saves, collection use, premium exports

So Google receives a real, substantive page. A free visitor sees enough to understand and desire the product. A member receives the complete utility.

The rule I’d use is:

> **Index the editorial record; gate the collectible utility.**
> 

That captures the useful part of Gemini’s “media authority” recommendation without turning Fandom Vibes into a public mirror of image-search results.
