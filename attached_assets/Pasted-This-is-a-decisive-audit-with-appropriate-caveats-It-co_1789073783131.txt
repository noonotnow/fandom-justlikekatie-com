This is a **decisive audit**, with appropriate caveats. It confirms that repetition is not one bug; it is an interacting retrieval-and-curation failure.

## What the numbers establish

- **Exact retrieval repetition:** 10 of 61 consecutive comparisons, or **16.4%**, returned the identical digest set.
- **Severe overlap:** 13 of 61, or **21.3%**, had at least 70% overlap. Assuming exact repeats are included in those 13, this is not additive.
- **Provider concentration:** Google Images supplied **97.8%** of query batches—314 of 321.
- **Promise attrition:** `coreSatisfied` rejected **68.3%** of retained candidate placements.
- **Visual proof sample:** **80%** of sampled failures were visually useful; **45%** were direct core false negatives. Because the sample was stratified, these percentages prove the failure mode exists but do not estimate its population prevalence.
- **Hidden inventory:** rejected rank 4–10 batches contained about **18 clean placements per batch** on average. That is substantial inaccessible inventory, although its usefulness remains unknown.
- **Missing composition opportunity:** 120 viable 4–8-image families across 65 runs means the nine-image Event threshold is not an edge case. The system encountered roughly **1.85 such families per affected run**.

## The counterfactual works

The Cold Jade board makes the composition problem immediately visible.

Its coherence comes from:

- the white, silver, pale-blue palette;
- controlled posture and restrained expression;
- repeated flowing costume language;
- cool exterior and shadowed architectural settings;
- a consistent celestial-authority character.

But the images are not performing one identical function. The board includes:

- close facial evidence;
- seated composure;
- full-body silhouette;
- environmental presence;
- direct confrontation;
- movement and atmospheric framing.

The sameness creates **continuity**, while framing and expression create variation. Breaking this into unrelated sources merely to satisfy diversity would likely weaken the collectible/editorial effect.

## The most important product conclusion

`coreSatisfied` is being asked to decide a visual question using incomplete textual evidence. A metadata-derived score can be a useful confidence signal, but it should not be a hard proxy for visual truth—especially when it controls 70% of promise scoring.

Likewise, “Event” should not mean:

> Nine surviving images must all belong to one automatically detected family.
> 

A more faithful model is:

> A dominant coherent family forms the board’s visual spine, while compatible supporting or connective images complete the argument.
> 

## Recommended intervention order

Keep the interventions isolated so their effects remain measurable.

### 1. Repair observability first

Before changing behavior, prospectively retain:

- cache-hit and cache-bypass status;
- candidate identities and safe visual references for rank 4–10 batches;
- pre/post-deduplication family membership;
- exact reason for every candidate’s attrition;
- query and ladder-rung provenance;
- whether an image supplied core, supporting, or connective evidence.

Without this, improvements could merely move losses into another invisible stage.

### 2. Test retrieval changes independently

Run controlled tests for:

- diversified query jobs rather than adjective variations;
- genuine cache bypass during refresh;
- provider diversification;
- a reserve beyond the top three batches;
- ranking based on unique useful evidence rather than raw batch volume.

Do not alter promise scoring in the same experiment.

### 3. Recalibrate promise fulfillment

Test a model in which:

- metadata supplies evidence rather than a hard verdict;
- visual review can rescue metadata-poor candidates;
- core, supporting, connective, contradictory, and irrelevant roles are separate;
- promise fulfillment is evaluated both per card and across the complete board;
- only contradiction or true unusability causes automatic exclusion.

The existing rejected-image sample should become the beginning of a regression set.

### 4. Add dominant-family composition

Test at least three board structures:

- **Compiled:** broadly independent evidence.
- **Event:** one sufficiently rich coherent family.
- **Dominant family + support:** perhaps 4–8 family images completed by compatible supporting cards.

Within Event-oriented modes, deduplicate photographs—not wardrobe, setting, lighting, or editorial continuity.

### 5. Tighten recurrence only after improving supply

Lowering the seven-of-nine threshold now could increase failed editions or rescue use because the upstream pool remains constrained. First improve retrieval and candidate survival; then set recurrence policy against the healthier pool.

## What is proven versus still unknown

**Proven:**

- retrieval output materially repeats;
- promise logic produces genuine visual false negatives;
- the top-three boundary discards substantial clean inventory;
- viable sub-nine families occur frequently;
- dominant-family-plus-support is aesthetically credible.

**Still unknown:**

- cache versus provider stability as the cause of exact repeats;
- population-wide false-negative rate;
- visual usefulness of historical rank 4–10 batches;
- the optimal family/support ratio;
- how much each intervention independently reduces published recurrence.

This is enough to authorize controlled experiments—not a blanket rewrite. Replit also handled the epistemics correctly: it distinguished confirmed funnel loss from unreviewable historical quality and did not mutate the system during diagnosis. That is the audit behavior you originally asked for.
