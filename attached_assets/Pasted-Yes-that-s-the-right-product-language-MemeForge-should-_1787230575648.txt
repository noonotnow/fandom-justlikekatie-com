Yes — that’s the right product language:

## MemeForge should be a **quippy reaction translator / angle generator**

Not:

> “AI generates a funny Middle-earth scene.”
> 

But:

> “MemeForge finds the sharp fandom-native angle and compresses it into a sendable reaction.”
> 

The output should feel closer to:

```
Prompt:
not wanting to go to work on Friday

Angle:
Mordor Commute

Card:
ONE DOES NOT SIMPLY

CLOCK IN ON A FRIDAY
```

or:

```
Prompt:
Sam and Frodo funny

Angle:
loyal friend refuses to let you spiral alone

Card:
FRODO: I CAN’T DO THIS, SAM

SAM: GOOD. WE’RE DOING IT TOGETHER.
```

## Product distinction

I’d describe it as:

> **MemeForge is a fandom angle generator.**
Give it a messy situation, character dynamic, or half-formed joke. It finds the Middle-earth reaction angle and turns it into a compact share card.
> 

Or more punchy:

> **Say the moment badly. MemeForge finds the bit.**
> 

That is maybe the cleanest tagline.

## The generator should optimize for

1. **Quip**
    - Short, sharp, instantly readable.
2. **Angle**
    - The recognizable social/fandom mapping:
    - “work = Mordor”
    - “friend support = Samwise loyalty”
    - “boundary = You Shall Not Pass”
    - “too many opinions = Council of Elrond”
    - “me vs me = Gollum debate”
3. **Reaction utility**
    - Would someone actually send this in a chat?
    - Could it work as a reaction image without a paragraph of context?
4. **Middle-earth flavor**
    - Character/scene/quote rhythm should carry the joke.
    - But no lore novella. No rabbits unless the rabbits are the joke.

## Acceptance test language

For future Replit tasks, I’d state:

> MemeForge should behave less like a general joke writer and more like a quippy reaction translator. It should identify the reusable social angle first, then output concise two-line card copy. If the result needs explanation to be funny, it failed.
> 

That’s the bar.
