Yes—you caught that I caught it. 😂 None of the private operator machinery should be repackaged as “premium membership.”
First Watch Journal belongs in Fandom Vibes
I’d argue for keeping it there because it is fandom-native editorial creation:
Watching and reacting to a drama
Sealed predictions
Episode boundaries
Spoiler-safe receipts
Veteran testimony
Later verdicts
Turning the experience into public fandom content
Creator OS may eventually receive the finished posts for distribution, but it should not own the underlying first-watch experience. The journal is part of how Fandom Vibes creates its editorial universe.
So the Operator Console could have:
Release Desk
Actor Preflight Lab
First Watch Journal
​
“Field Journal” can remain the broader category if you expect trope observations and other research beyond first watches. Otherwise, First Watch Journal is more legible and charming.
Court Rulings should not receive serious navigation weight
If it is meant to be a cute easter egg, it should behave like one—not like a major department in enterprise software.
Possibilities:
A tiny gavel icon in the Operator Console
“Recent rulings” tucked into a footer
A secret chamber opened from a decision receipt
A playful “Court is now in session” overlay
Deep links from relevant precedents
It can preserve real policy history without sitting beside Release Desk as though operators begin every morning by opening Supreme Court.
Premium should remain undecided
You’re right not to create a paywall merely because a feature could be premium. Membership value should emerge from what fans repeatedly want—not from relocating internal tools behind a subscription.
The two plausible future premium ideas are:
Personal curator training
A member teaches their own curator:
More Event versus Compiled
Favorite looks and characters
Preferred intensity
Personal anti-Vibes
“More like this” feedback
That must remain separate from canonical curator training:
Katie/operator feedback can calibrate the official curator.
Member feedback personalizes that member’s results.
Optional anonymized aggregate learning requires explicit consent.
One member cannot accidentally retrain the public Daily Drop.
Expanded Grid Builder evidence
Members might eventually want:
More than the public nine
Alternate approved cards
Expanded character/look clusters
More saved arrangements
Advanced export treatments
Personal Event/Compiled editions
That could become premium, but it does not need to be the boundary now. First prove that people enjoy collecting and building.
The simple structure for now
Fandom Vibes
├── Public
│   ├── Daily Drop
│   ├── Archive
│   └── Native sharing
├── Personal
│   ├── Collection
│   └── Grid Builder
└── Operator Console
    ├── Release Desk
    ├── Actor Preflight Lab
    ├── First Watch / Field Journal
    └── Court Rulings — easter egg
​
No forced premium distinction. No Workstation dependency. No turning audit debris into a member benefit.
Build the delightful behavior first; decide the membership boundary after fans show you what they value enough to want more of.
