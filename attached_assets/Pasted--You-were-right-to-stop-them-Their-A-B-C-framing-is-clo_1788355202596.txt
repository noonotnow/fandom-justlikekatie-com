**You were right to stop them.** Their A/B/C framing is close, but it still treats the main decision as “where should this tab go?” The larger decision is:

> **Who is doing what, with which assets, and at what stage of the publishing lifecycle?**
> 

I would not choose A, B, or C exactly as written. I’d propose a **D**.

# Recommended architecture

## Fandom Vibes

The overall product world and brand.

It can contain multiple editorial products or universes:

- **Vibe Atlas**
- **MemeForge**
- Future fandom formats

These are not “workbenches.” They are the experiences and editorial worlds people come to consume and participate in.

## Public Vibe Atlas

The published experience:

- Star of the Day
- Daily Drop
- Archive
- Public Vibe pages
- Public actor pages
- Shareable editions

This contains only released material. No audit language, rejected images, promise counters, or operator receipts.

## Member Collection

The member’s personal library:

- Saved cards
- Saved editions
- Favorite actors and Vibes
- Personal rankings
- Taste history
- Event/Compiled/Hybrid preferences

This is memory and identity: **what the member loves**.

## Member Workstation

This is where premium members **do things** with the published universe:

- Build personal grids from cleared/released material
- Rearrange cards
- Create personal Event or Compiled sets
- Compare editions
- Export or share member creations
- Maintain personal taste profiles
- Potentially unlock alternate approved cards or extended editorial sets

Workstation is not “Admin with fewer buttons.” Its value is personal authorship and play.

> **Collection is what I kept. Workstation is what I can make.**
> 

That is strong premium membership value.

## Admin access

**Admin is an access boundary**, not a destination and not a product identity.

It unlocks the private Operator Console. You can eventually replace the visible “Admin” tab with something subtler like **Operator**, but that naming decision does not need to block the structure.

## Operator Console

The private internal shell for running Fandom Vibes.

Its workspaces should correspond to actual jobs—not every individual data panel.

### 1. Actor Preflight

**Question:** What pairings deserve to become possible releases?

Contains:

- Actor profiles
- Query ladders
- Event-family detection
- Curator proposals
- Rescue boards
- Calibration receipts
- Pairing approval
- Curator learning diagnostics

Its output is an immutable approved publication candidate.

Actor Preflight should **not own the release inventory**. It hands approved work downstream.

### 2. Release Desk

**Question:** What can ship, what needs work, and what ships next?

This should replace the proposed standalone “Release Inventory” tab and absorb the useful parts of PLAN schedule.

Release Inventory becomes the first view inside Release Desk:

- **Inventory**
- **Production**
- **Schedule**
- **Published**
- **Held/retired**

The flow becomes:

> Approved in Preflight → enters Release Desk → assets prepared → enhanced → exported → scheduled → published
> 

That is much more durable than making “inventory” and “schedule” unrelated sibling tabs.

### 3. Field Journal

Editorial observation and research:

- First-watch notes
- Reaction capture
- Prediction receipts
- Trope observations
- Story seeds

### 4. Court Rulings

Policy and precedent:

- Editorial decisions
- Rights decisions
- Naming precedents
- Product rules
- Exceptions
- Reversals

It is governance and institutional memory—not a daily production queue.

# What should be retired

“Retired” should not mean deleted. Different things require different retirement scopes.

## Hold this exact edition

The board remains approved but is temporarily removed from scheduling.

Examples:

- Needs better image files
- Enhancement incomplete
- Too close to another actor’s release
- Seasonal timing
- Copy unfinished

It remains in Release Desk under **Held**.

## Retire this edition

This exact nine is no longer a publication candidate.

- Preserve its receipt.
- Preserve its approval history.
- Record a reason.
- Optionally link to the replacement edition.

It should not reappear in the active queue.

## Retire this actor × Vibe pairing

The existing editions remain historical, but the pairing is no longer suggested for new Daily Drops.

This does not retire the actor or the Vibe Pack globally.

## Retire a Vibe Pack

A larger editorial decision:

- Remove it from future curation
- Preserve published editions in the archive
- Preserve member saves
- Display a successor name if it was renamed or merged

For example, Shattered Beauty should behave as a renamed predecessor of **Professionally Devastated**, not as if its history never existed.

## Rights withdrawal

This is different from editorial retirement.

If an asset must be removed for rights reasons:

- Remove or replace the public image
- Remove it from Workstation
- Preserve the private receipt and reason
- Show an appropriate public tombstone or revised edition
- Do not silently rewrite publication history

# What belongs in Release Desk

Each exact publication candidate should have clear stages:

1. **Editorially approved**
2. **Exact nine frozen**
3. **Source/provenance reviewed**
4. **Production assets secured**
5. **Enhancement scrub complete**
6. **Final render verified**
7. **Copy complete**
8. **Schedule eligible**
9. **Scheduled**
10. **Published**
11. **Held or retired**

That makes the Star of the Day problem visible. A board can be editorially excellent while still being:

- Missing exportable assets
- Awaiting enhancement
- Missing copy
- Too close to another release by the same actor
- Blocked by rights review

Those are production states—not reasons to send it backward into Actor Preflight.

# Where Dylan’s board belongs

Dylan’s preflight can produce and display complete proposed grids even if the automated hero gate fails.

Once you approve an exact nine with both confirmations:

- That frozen board enters **Release Desk → Inventory**
- Preflight retains the audit and calibration problem
- Release Desk owns whether the edition is production-ready
- Workstation sees nothing until the edition or its assets are published/cleared

The curator’s failure cannot remove the approved edition from Release Desk.

# Premium membership boundary

The cleanest principle is:

> **Operators decide what enters the canon. Members decide what the canon means to them.**
> 

Premium members can:

- Save
- Sort
- Compare
- Remix
- Build
- Express preferences
- Export personal editions
- Receive deeper or alternate published material

Premium members should not automatically see:

- Unreleased source pools
- Rejected images
- Audit diagnostics
- Query ladders
- Rights notes
- Operator disagreements
- Unapproved rescue boards

That material is not premium content. It is internal production machinery.
