Vibing Now · Against the Current · Episodes 34–38

Being Remembered, Not Being Discovered

A borrowed birthday, a rebellion, and the birthday he remembers: Episodes 34–38 make Jinqi’s care more specific—and Lanxiang’s choice more complicated.

Contains spoilers through Episode 38 only · Current-state reading

Spoiler boundary: This installment stops at the end of Episode 38. No previews, later episodes, novel material, or endgame information.

The birthday that belongs to someone else

Jinqi asks whether Lanxiang resents him for forcing her to marry him. She points out that he arranged her life without first finding out what she thought or wanted. His question arrives after the decision; her answer makes that order impossible to overlook.

Then he throws himself into celebrating her birthday. There is a wishing tree, and his wish is to spend the day with her. Hers is to find where her family’s remains are buried.

The celebration belongs to the identity she is living under. He is trying to make the day hers, while the date itself reminds her of the life she cannot openly claim. His enthusiasm and her grief occupy the same birthday without becoming the same feeling.

He lets her get drunk and have a tantrum without making her feel bad about it. His birthday girl is not enjoying her birthday, and he lets that be true. She does not have to make the day pleasant for him before he will stay beside her.

A mule is not a marriage decree

When a soldier’s failure to invite the opera singer alerts Lanxiang that something is wrong, she goes to check on Jinqi. Once the rebellion leaves him badly injured, she sends help to him instead of accepting an escort home.

Her argument does not stop at her husband. The people in danger are owed safety before she is owed an easy return. She goes toward him without forgetting everyone else who needs help.

She comes to collect him on a mule, carrot included. He is coughing up blood; she is getting him out. The romance has acquired transport, and it is considerably less grand than an imperial decree.

Jinqi can be charming while bleeding. He even turns a shooting lesson into an excuse to get close to her. There is room for the ridiculousness because she has already done something more consequential: she came back for him.

What she chooses to protect

That choice widens in Episode 36. Lanxiang encounters the first wife being assaulted, and the two women act together. Their alliance is made in danger, where being a current wife or a former wife offers neither woman enough protection.

Xingtang’s death gives the encounter its terrible weight. Lanxiang addresses her as Xingtang, not through a marital title. For a moment, the woman is not being sorted by her relationship to a husband. She is being met by her own name.

For Lanxiang, living under a borrowed name and celebrating a borrowed birthday, that distinction is painfully close. She can recognize another woman as herself while keeping so much of herself hidden.

Her protection of Jinqi also becomes less public. She burns the evidence against him without telling him. He witnesses the act, but she has not arranged it as a demonstration for him. Something she could have held over him is gone, and she does not ask him to be grateful.

The rescue brought her back to him. The burning shows him what she will do when she is not asking for anything in return. He sees care that has not arrived with a claim attached.

In Episode 37, someone else’s attempt to control Jinqi gives him a way to recognize his own coercion. His understanding of Lanxiang’s anger changes: she did not have a crush on Scholar Han. She objected to being married off like a piece of land.

A rival would have made the explanation easier. He could have blamed another man for the distance between them. Instead, he has to understand that the injury was his decision to make: her wanting had not been allowed to matter.

She has protected him without using that protection to bind him to her. Now he begins to understand why binding her to him was the injury. By the time he takes her to the graves, care means something more particular than being useful to one another.

What she thought had been abandoned

Jinqi takes her to a remote burial site in Episode 38. He had the Shen family buried after their execution. He also saved some of their servants, who care for the grave.

He tells her how his family let the Shens down and how he could not let it go. He brings her here to show her a responsibility he has carried privately. The people buried in this remote place have remained part of his life, even when she could not find them in hers.

This is what makes the revelation more intimate than a husband fulfilling a wish. She thought of the Lins through their failure toward her family. Now she is standing before the care Jinqi took after that failure. The abandonment she has lived with was not the end of his relationship to the Shens.

He cannot bring her family back. But he had them buried with dignity, and he kept people alive to tend their grave. What she feared had been abandoned entirely had been entrusted to someone’s care. His care.

The place changes what she can believe about him. It holds his family’s failure and his own answer to it together. He is not simply doing a kindness for her now; she is discovering something he did when she was not there to receive it.

Her burning of the evidence begins to echo differently here. Both have protected something precious to the other without announcing it as a favor. She has seen what he preserved before she could ask him for it.

She tells him the story of her benefactors, leaving out the identity swap. Her account is incomplete; her grief is not. He is standing beside her at the place she wished she could find, and she can let him see what it means to her.

The birthday he kept

She has forgotten her original birthday. He tells her that the day of their visit is Shen Jialan’s birthday. She cries because she forgot and he remembered for her.

In Episode 34, he wanted to spend her birthday with her. Here, he gives her the date she had stopped remembering as her own. She does not have to produce it for him. He has kept it.

She has vowed never to forget the person who buried her family with dignity. That person is Jinqi. The husband imposed on her is also the man who kept this part of her life from being erased. Her feelings have to make room for both.

They bow together, with wedding vows playing over the bows. Before her family, those vows begin to feel chosen. She is not only accepting his care; she is standing beside him as their marriage takes on a meaning she can answer for herself.

The birthday he celebrated was borrowed. The birthday he remembers is hers. And here, before the family he preserved for her, they bow together.
