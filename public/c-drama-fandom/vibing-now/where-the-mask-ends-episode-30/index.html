Where the Mask Ends

The finale.

Ending spoilers · Episode 30

Spoiler boundary: This installment covers Episode 30, the finale, and includes ending spoilers. No novel material.

Han Liao storms the Duke’s house. Wei Rao sends A Man out with a message: the two hundred pigeons have already gone to the common people, and if even one leaves the city, word will spread that the crown prince killed his father for the throne. The garrisons and the feudal lords would turn on him. That buys her a private meeting, and Han Liao comes in alone.

The ring

Han Liao threatens Lady Li and Liu Shuo: marry him, or Lady Li dies first. Wei Rao pretends to agree. While he celebrates, she goes for him with a knife. He blocks it, but the ring on her other hand hides a poisoned needle, and it pricks his neck. He collapses. She pours oil around him, tells him what she felt for Lu Zhuo, and drops a lit candle, promising to kill him first and the crown prince after.

Then the coffin moves, and Lu Zhuo climbs out alive. When Han Liao tried to separate him from Qi Zhongkai, Lu Zhuo used a staged death to disappear and let the court believe he was gone. Han Liao dies on his sword.

The seal

Before his illness worsened, the Emperor gave Lady Li the imperial seal for safekeeping. The crown prince demands it and promises to spare Wei Rao in return. Lady Li chooses her daughter without a moment’s hesitation. The crown prince would let her go, but Wei Shu orders an archer to shoot as Lady Li runs to Wei Rao. Lu Zhuo looses two arrows at once, one into the soldier and one into Wei Shu. Following Lu Zhuo’s plan, Qi Zhongkai has already captured Zheng Tan, and the coup collapses. Having heard how far her mother was willing to go for her, Wei Rao finally calls Lady Li “mother,” and Lady Li weeps with joy.

Wei Shu slips away in the confusion and runs straight into Hongxian and Xiao. Hongxian intends to put her through everything she did to those innocent girls before she is executed. The Emperor strips the crown prince of his rank, makes him a commoner and orders him to take his own life, and names Liu Shuo crown prince. Only then does the deposed heir understand that his father always wanted Liu Shuo, the son with clean hands, and that he himself was only ever a stepping stone. The Emperor makes Wei Rao Grand Protector Princess and tells Lu Zhuo to serve the new heir.

Three years later

The Emperor dies the following early summer, and Liu Shuo takes the throne. Three years later, Prince Fu lures him into woods full of wild bears. Liu Shuo is thrown from his horse and badly hurt, and Wei Rao reaches the palace in time to hear his last words. Weeping, Xie Hualou names Prince Fu, on the day before the feudal lords are due at court. For three years Wei Rao had urged Liu Shuo to deal with Prince Fu, but he kept his father’s dying wish to treat his last brother well. Now she orders Prince Fu buried with him.

Hongxian, the only one thinking clearly, places Wei Rao’s hand on her belly: she is carrying Liu Shuo’s child. The lords would likely back Prince Rong, the Grand Empress Dowager’s younger son, so Wei Rao confines the old woman under cover of mourning and prepares to meet the lords herself.

In the final scene she climbs the steps of Xuanshi Hall one at a time while everyone watches, looks back, and resolves to make them bow.

The title has been waiting thirty episodes to land. The mask ended back in Episode 16, when the girl everyone called an impostor turned out to have the only real claim to her name. What the finale adds is what she does with it: not a quiet happy ending, but a woman who started out hauling night soil walking up the steps of the hall where emperors make decisions.
