Vibing Now · Against the Current · Episodes 30–31

Lanxiang Comes Home. Then She Shows Jinqi How She Works.

The return to her childhood home and the fight for Ji’er’s freedom belong to the same movement: Lanxiang has a history, judgment, and work of her own.

Current vibe check · Contains spoilers through Episode 31 only

Spoiler boundary: This installment stops at the end of Episode 31. No previews, later episodes, novel material, or endgame information.

Episodes 30–31 bring Lanxiang back to her childhood home and let her demonstrate how she wants to act in the present. The return is emotionally complicated; the rescue is practical. Jinqi is involved in both, but involvement is not the same as deciding what either should mean.

Episode 30: Coming home is not the same as starting over

Episode 30 begins with the bath scene's continuation. Jinqi offers the spectacle; Lanxiang offers practical care. She dresses him, tells him not to catch cold, and calls for ginger soup. It is funny and tender without making the decision to bring her to the capital hers.

Then she walks into her childhood home.

For Jinqi, this is where they are arriving. For Lanxiang, it is a place she has already lost. The same rooms cannot mean the same thing to them.

Her reaction to the child makes that difference visible. A memory interrupts the present; Jinqi helps ground her in who the child actually is. His care matters, even though he cannot understand all the history behind her response.

She is not arriving in the capital as a blank slate. Before she can turn to anyone else’s future, she has to stand inside the rooms of her own missing past.

Episode 30: Helping her is not the same as taking over

Ji’er's freedom gives that difference another form. Jinqi offers a solution through the power available to him. Lanxiang wants to work out another way.

His offer can be sincere without being the answer she wants. That is what makes the disagreement interesting: he wants to help, but she does not want every problem in her life solved by his position.

She also brings the marriage back into the argument. Would he have acted without consulting her or her parents if they had not come from a servant household? What he calls choosing her across a class boundary still leaves her asking why her own choice was so easy to bypass.

His challenge—whether she can win Ji’er back without status or power—makes Episode 31's negotiation an answer to a question Episode 30 has already posed. The release is not an isolated demonstration of cleverness. It shows him another way of getting something done.

Episode 31: Getting her friend back is only the first step

Zhao Xingtang makes Ji’er's release a negotiation, then offers a challenge: land the shot, win her friend's freedom.

Lanxiang lets Xingtang underestimate her. Her apparent incompetence makes the challenge look safe for the woman offering it.

It isn't.

She lands the shot. Xingtang keeps her word.

The pleasure of the scene is not simply that Lanxiang is better at the game than expected. She has read the person setting the terms. She can see where pride, curiosity, and self-interest leave room for negotiation.

That does not make Xingtang's power over Ji’er benign. Keeping a bargain is meaningful; it is not the same as never having needed one.

Then the maids gather around the table to read about getting Ji’er registered.

The rescue does not end with a triumphant embrace. It continues as paperwork, shared reading, and a practical question about how freedom becomes something a person can live with.

This is the show's women-helping-women thread at its most concrete. The group is not there merely to admire Lanxiang's cleverness. They are working through what comes next together.

Lanxiang can win the shot. Getting Ji’er into a life she can actually live takes more than marksmanship. Around the reading table, the other women become participants in that work—not an audience assembled to applaud an exceptional heroine.

Lanxiang also says she had planned to write plays before marrying Jinqi. Her plans are not an accessory to his romance. There was already a life she wanted to make.

One affectionate costume confession before we move on: Jinqi's green robe, broad white collar, and fur-trimmed sleeves have given me an association I cannot undo. Buddy the Elf. I love this show, I am invested in this romance, and apparently my brain can accommodate both of those things while insisting that he is dressed for an exceptionally elegant Christmas special.

The current: she has work of her own

The house holds the life Lanxiang lost. The reading table holds work she can still choose to do.

She cannot explain everything the house means to her. She can insist that her judgment matters, recover her friend through a negotiation of her own, and tell Jinqi that writing plays was already part of the life she wanted.

That is a different question from whether he is willing to protect her. Can he make room for work, relationships, and decisions that do not begin with him?
