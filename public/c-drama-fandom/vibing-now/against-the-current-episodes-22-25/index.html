Vibing Now · Against the Current · Episodes 22–25

Against the Current Gives Lanxiang Freedom, Then Sends the State to Collect It

She faces a reputation trap, a trafficking plot, and a wedding assassination attempt. The document that finally defeats her is a marriage decree.

Current vibe check · Contains spoilers through Episode 25 only · Reviewed and published September 28, 2026

Spoiler boundary: This installment stops at the end of Episode 25. No previews, later episodes, novel material, or endgame information.

There is a brief moment at the end of Episode 21 when Lanxiang appears to have accomplished something dangerously close to freedom.

She has left the Lin household. She is earning money at the painting shop. Her parents have a home. Lin Jinqi no longer has a legitimate administrative reason to appear wherever she happens to be standing, which has not stopped him from trying but has at least made the behavior visible.

Episodes 22 through 25 respond by demonstrating exactly how many institutions consider Lanxiang’s autonomy temporary.

First, her reputation is made available for public destruction. Then her body is made available for sale. Then a wedding becomes an assassination site. Lanxiang survives every private trap through attention, improvisation, and the network of ordinary people who remember what she has done for them.

Then Lin Jinqi asks the emperor for her hand.

The show has spent four episodes proving that Lanxiang can outthink coercion when coercion still needs to disguise itself as scandal, commerce, or conspiracy. It ends this movement by confronting her with coercion carrying the imperial seal.

Episodes 22–25

Freedom is only as secure as the story people agree to tell about you

Zheng Manyi’s first weapon is not a sword. It is a scene.

Lanxiang is drugged and placed with Han Liang so that other people can discover them together. The scheme does not require either of them to have done anything. It only requires the correct bodies in the correct room and a society prepared to convert an image into a verdict.

That is the particular vulnerability of Lanxiang’s new freedom. She may no longer be legally owned, but her ability to work, marry, move, and remain respectable still depends on other people believing a socially acceptable story about her body.

The trap assumes appearances will become law before Lanxiang can speak.

It also reveals how quickly Jinqi’s feelings have become public information. Manyi identifies Lanxiang as the object of his attention through a poem and an orchid. Lanxiang, meanwhile, treats the orchid with the sentimentality of a woman who has recently paid rent. Sell the plant. Take the hundred taels. Split the money with the people who gave it to her. Romance is welcome to submit an invoice.

The comedy matters because Lanxiang is not living inside Jinqi’s emotional timeline. He is discovering devotion. She is building solvency.

Episodes 22–25

Lanxiang’s kindness keeps returning as infrastructure

The kidnapping and Qingyan House movement bring the terror shadowing Episode 1 back into the foreground. Lanxiang has spent years living under the identity that saved her from the Jiaofang Bureau. Now she is sold into another institution designed to convert vulnerable women into revenue.

The brothel madam is almost operatically cruel, but the structure around that cruelty is ordinary. Lanxiang is treated as inventory. Resistance does not disqualify her from sale; it merely changes the category of buyer expected to find her suffering appealing.

Her attention finds openings. She improvises to escape. But this is not another scene in which the brilliant heroine rescues herself while everybody else arrives late with a horse.

Ji’er acts.

Earlier kindness has created a relationship strong enough for Ji’er to take Lanxiang’s place at terrible personal risk. Han Liang, Furong, the Xu family, and the people who know Lanxiang begin moving because her absence matters to them. They do not share rank, wealth, or official authority. What they share is accumulated reciprocity.

Powerful men build networks through office, marriage, and command. Lanxiang builds one by treating supposedly disposable people as people.

When the formal system sells her, the informal one becomes a rescue system.

This is why Jinqi’s failure to recognize Ji’er after the escape lands so sharply. He is furious about what has happened. He can threaten the madam and search the premises. He can care about servants as a category and still fail to see the particular servant standing in front of him.

His privilege is not that he feels nothing. It is that the social world has never required the same quality of attention from him that survival requires from Lanxiang.

Episodes 22–25

Performance becomes counter-surveillance

Lanxiang does not return from captivity ready to disappear quietly.

She recognizes that Zheng Manyi’s power depends on controlling which story becomes public. So she answers with theater.

The wedding performance turns costume, music, hidden access, mistaken identity, and overheard arrogance into political machinery. Lanxiang places Manyi in a setting where she feels protected enough to speak freely and arranges for authority to be listening from somewhere she cannot see.

The woman who tried to manufacture evidence against Lanxiang becomes the author of her own evidence.

Meanwhile, the assassination plot operates through the same logic at a larger scale. The supposed Crown Prince onstage is not the real Crown Prince. Visibility is decoy; concealment is strategy. The wedding guests believe they understand who is performing, who is watching, and who is vulnerable. Almost everyone is wrong.

Lanxiang and Jinqi are working through parallel methods without working as a conventional romantic unit. Both understand that power can be defeated by controlling who sees what and when. Both use performance to make an enemy act inside a false understanding of the room.

The difference is that Jinqi’s faction can redirect soldiers and princes. Lanxiang has to turn the decorative spaces available to women—opera, costume, gossip, reputation—into an intelligence network.

She does not possess less political intelligence. She is simply required to hide it in prettier containers.

Episodes 22–25

Lin Jinqi mistakes permanent access for romantic certainty

By Episode 25, Han Liang is prepared to propose.

He represents an ordinary future at human scale: neighboring walls, shared work, family approval, conversation, and the possibility that affection might grow because Lanxiang is free to remain or leave.

Jinqi responds in the most Lin Jinqi manner available.

He asks the emperor to bestow Lanxiang upon him.

From his perspective, this is commitment. He is willing to cross a severe class boundary, oppose his family, spend imperial favor, and accept whatever punishment follows. He does not consider Lanxiang beneath him.

But Lanxiang has only just stopped belonging to his household.

The decree does not prove that his feelings are false. It proves that sincerity and entitlement can occupy the same decision. Faced with the possibility that Lanxiang might choose another future, Jinqi does not ask how to become someone she would choose. He obtains authority that makes the competing choice impossible.

The Xu parents understand the danger immediately. They do not experience the decree as the fairy-tale elevation of their daughter. They see her being returned to the household from which she has only just escaped.

Lanxiang accepts the news with almost no visible grievance in front of them. That composure is not consent. It is another act of care: she contains her fear so the people who saved her will not be destroyed by their inability to protect her from the emperor.

The strongest man in her emotional orbit has used the strongest document in the realm to guarantee that she cannot build a life beyond his reach.

Romantically, the audit is not going well.

Episodes 22–25

The current: freedom on revocable terms

Episodes 22 through 25 expand Against the Current beyond household politics without abandoning the household’s central lesson: a woman’s choices remain vulnerable wherever another institution can claim authority over her body, reputation, labor, or future.

Lanxiang defeats the traps that require her to be less observant than her enemies. She survives because kindness has become infrastructure and because supposedly powerless people recognize one another clearly.

She cannot defeat the marriage decree because obedience is built into the document.

Jinqi has spent the drama learning that Lanxiang cannot be managed like a household variable.

His response is to submit the problem to a higher administrative authority.

Strategically, this is extremely effective.

Romantically, the next installment has questions.
