Cultivation field guide

Cultivation levels, explained

There is no official cultivation ladder. There is a famous one, and once you know it, a lot of subtitles start making sense.

See the ladderSee today’s Atlas drop

The short answer

Cultivation levels, also called realms, are named stages of power and transformation in Chinese fantasy stories.

The most widely copied ladder, popularized by web novels such as A Record of a Mortal’s Journey to Immortality, runs from Qi Refining through Foundation Establishment, Core Formation, and Nascent Soul toward Mahayana and ascension. Many dramas borrow pieces of it; many invent their own.

Treat the ladder below as a reference map, not a rulebook. Each story is responsible for teaching you its own system. For what cultivation is in the first place, start with the cultivation guide.

The common ladder

#LevelCommon English namesWhat the stage usually means
1炼气 liàn qìQi Refining, Qi CondensationSensing and gathering spiritual energy. The entry point that separates cultivators from ordinary people.
2筑基 zhù jīFoundation Establishment, Foundation BuildingBuilding a stable base that can hold real power. Often the first true gate, and the first big lifespan gain.
3结丹 jié dān / 金丹 jīn dānCore Formation, Golden CoreCondensing energy into an inner core. Losing or damaging a core is a recurring crisis.
4元婴 yuán yīngNascent SoulA soul-self forms that can, in many stories, survive the body’s destruction.
5化神 huà shénSpirit Transformation, Deity TransformationPower begins to touch the laws of the world itself. Rare enough that whole regions may have none.
6炼虚 liàn xūVoid Refining, Void TrainingMastery over space and emptiness.
7合体 hé tǐBody Integration, Body FusionBody, soul, and the world’s energy merge.
8大乘 dà chéngMahayana, Great AscensionThe peak of the mortal realm, one step from leaving it.

Many stories add a final crossing, a tribulation (渡劫, dù jié), before a cultivator can ascend. Most levels are also split into sub-stages, commonly early, middle, late, and peak (sometimes “great perfection”).

Why the English names never match

The Chinese terms are fairly stable; translations are not. 结丹 can appear as Core Formation, Golden Core, or Dan Formation depending on the novel, the subtitle team, or the platform. If your subtitles use a name you don’t recognize, check the Chinese term, not the English one.

How dramas use (and ignore) the ladder

Web-novel adaptations

These keep more of the ladder, because the source novel uses rank as plot structure.

Romance-led xianxia

These often compress it. A breakthrough matters for what it costs the relationship, not for its number.

Immortal-court dramas

These frequently use status titles instead, such as 上仙 (shàngxiān) and 上神 (shàngshén). Those are ranks in a celestial hierarchy, not training stages.

Wuxia

Wuxia rarely uses levels at all. Skill and internal force matter, but there is no ladder to immortality.

In The Untamed, for example, a cultivator’s golden core is the seat of their power, which is why the story treats any threat to a core so seriously. You don’t need the full ladder to feel that weight.

A three-part rank reading lens

A rank is a claim the story makes about what effort earns.

LabelWhat does this drama call the stage, and does it explain the name or assume you know it?
GapHow far apart are the characters in a confrontation, and does the story honor that gap or break it?
StoryWhat is the rank really saying: patience rewarded, privilege inherited, or a shortcut that will cost something later?

A level tells you what a character can do. The cultivation guide helps you read what they are becoming while they do it.

Common questions

What is the highest cultivation level?

It depends on the story. In the common ladder, Mahayana is the peak of the mortal world, followed by a tribulation and ascension. Many stories continue with immortal realms beyond that.

What is Nascent Soul?

The fourth stage of the common ladder: a soul-self forms that can often outlive the body. In many stories it is where a cultivator becomes a true power.

What is a golden core?

An inner core of condensed spiritual energy (金丹), formed at the third stage. Because power lives there, damage to a core is one of cultivation fiction’s biggest crises.

Do all C-dramas use these levels?

No. Some use part of the ladder, some use celestial rank titles, and some invent their own system entirely.
