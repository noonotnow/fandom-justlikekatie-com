Love Between Fairy and Devil / What to watch next

Recommendation authority · spoiler-light

Do not look for a duplicate. Choose the feeling you want to keep.

The right follow-up depends on whether you loved the cosmic romance, comic tenderness, moral reversals, flower-spirit fantasy, visual spectacle, or the discovery that another genre might be your real destination.

Choose your pathReturn to the guide

The short answer

Match the story’s emotional engine, then accept the tradeoff.

No drama can reproduce the exact combination of performances, music, production design, comedy, pacing, and character growth that made Love Between Fairy and Devil work for you. Recommendations become more accurate when they identify one quality to preserve and one difference to expect.

Use the paths below as editorial matches, not promises that every title has the same ending, tone, or relationship dynamic. Availability and subtitle options also vary by region and platform.

If you want darker cosmic romance: Till the End of the Moon

Choose this when the part you want to continue is the collision between love, feared identity, fate, and world-level catastrophe. It leans harder into suspicion, trauma, moral pressure, and the danger of trying to prevent a future by controlling a person in the present.

The match: xianxia scale, a male lead associated with destructive destiny, visually ambitious fantasy, and romance entangled with survival.

The tradeoff: the emotional atmosphere is more punishing and less buoyant. Do not expect the same ratio of comic warmth to darkness.

If you want flower-spirit fantasy and long-form fate: Ashes of Love

Choose this for an earlier popular xianxia built around a sheltered flower spirit, divine realms, romantic misunderstanding, family power, and the consequences of treating emotion as something that can be removed or managed.

The match: floral identity, immortal politics, a heroine whose emotional education shapes the plot, and love stretched across supernatural stakes.

The tradeoff: the storytelling is longer and more melodramatic, with different pacing and a more extended investment in misunderstanding.

If you want playful immortals and a romantic ensemble: The Starry Love

Choose this when humor, sisterhood, realm-crossing romance, and characters who complicate the identities assigned to them were central to your enjoyment.

The match: comic energy alongside real emotional consequence, contrasting realms, mistaken expectations, and relationships that revise political categories.

The tradeoff: its emotional architecture is distributed across an ensemble and sister relationship rather than concentrated in one transformative central bond.

If you want another romance shaped by repeated fate: Love You Seven Times

Choose this for a lighter, fate-forward xianxia whose central pair must encounter each other through multiple lives and changing identities. The structure turns reincarnation into a test of what persists when circumstances change.

The match: comic friction, heavenly systems, repeated romantic discovery, and visual fantasy.

The tradeoff: the episodic life structure creates a different rhythm from LBFAD’s sustained realm conflict and political transformation.

If the comedy and genre play mattered most: The Romance of Tiger and Rose

This is not a xianxia replacement. Choose it if what you loved was quick comic chemistry, a heroine disrupting a story’s expected rules, a dangerous male lead whose interpretation changes, and a romance that knows how to play with genre conventions.

The match: oppositional chemistry, humor, role reversal, and a heroine underestimated by the world around her.

The tradeoff: it uses a self-aware costume-romance premise rather than immortal realms, cultivation, or cosmic war.

If you discovered you like character transformation more than romance: Mysterious Lotus Casebook

Choose this detour when your favorite part was watching a feared or legendary identity become more complicated through companionship, restraint, and changing priorities.

The match: charismatic character work, humor beside grief, layered identities, and relationships that expose the limits of reputation.

The tradeoff: this is a wuxia mystery and friendship-centered journey, not a xianxia romance. The genre comparison guide explains the shift.

If you want a foundational epic romance: Eternal Love

Choose this when you want immortal identity, multiple lives, memory, duty, and romance unfolding at classic xianxia scale. It is useful for seeing how many genre expectations familiar to international viewers were established and circulated.

The match: cosmic romance, identity across lives, heavenly hierarchy, and the cost of immortal duty.

The tradeoff: its pacing, gender politics, and melodramatic structures belong to a different production moment and may feel less immediately streamlined.

TradeoffDecide what can change: pacing, genre, romance weight, ending tone, ensemble size, or production era.
NextChoose the title whose strengths match your priority—not the one with the longest list of surface similarities.

Not sure whether you want more xianxia?

Use the wuxia vs. xianxia vs. xuanhuan comparison. It separates immortal cosmology from the martial social world of wuxia and the freer invented systems often associated with xuanhuan.
