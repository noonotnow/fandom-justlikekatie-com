name: Vibing Now episode boundaries
description: Editorial review principle for publishing current-state drama readings without next-episode leaks.
---

An editor-approved article can still contain a plot event from beyond its advertised episode boundary. Check each consequential plot claim against episode-specific watch notes before publication, and obtain approval for any substantive removal or reassignment; a deploy preview alone is never source review.

**Why:** A draft marked ready for an Episodes 22–25 reading included a Zheng-family sentencing sequence placed in Episode 26 by the creator's watch notes. The creator approved removing that section from the earlier reading because the later article already addressed it.

**How to apply:** For each future Vibing Now installment, compare plot claims with the creator's watched/source material and declared endpoint, record the review date, and keep later-episode details out of earlier article bodies and discussions. Do not treat a spoiler-boundary disclaimer as proof the body is safe.

Boundary notices must describe episode coverage and excluded source categories, never named plot events or scene cutoffs—even when saying an event is absent.

**Why:** The creator flagged excluded-event wording as a spoiler itself and approved event-free episode-range notices instead.

**How to apply:** Keep warnings limited to the episode range, final episode, and exclusions such as later episodes, previews, novel material and endgame information. Keep plot analysis separate from the warning.

The creator keeps Against the Current viewing notes in Notion, as separate article records under “Vibing Now,” in the “Watch Notes” columns.

**Why:** The creator identified this as the source location for retrospective episode reviews; absence from the repository does not mean the notes are unavailable.

**How to apply:** Read the authorized Notion records before requesting uploads. Empty episode headings and article prose are not viewing evidence.

**Why:** The creator explicitly clarified the historical evidence limit after the available Notion records were checked.

**How to apply:** Do not keep requesting nonexistent original notes or reconstruct them as first-watch evidence. Distinguish later verification from original observations; obtain an explicit scope decision before substituting rewatch or alternative sources for a retrospective notes-based audit.

**Why:** The creator clarified that a rewatch label identifies recording context, not evidence from a later episode.

**How to apply:** Accept episode-specific rewatch observations as evidence for that episode. Check the episode supplying a detail, not the watch number; do not infer a spoiler risk or evidence gap merely because notes came from a second watch.
