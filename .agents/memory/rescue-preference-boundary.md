---
name: Rescue preference boundary
description: Distinguishes optional rescue preference from exact-board human publication approval in Actor Preflight.
---

A rescue-board preference is an immutable, point-in-time editorial receipt tied to a saved rescue arrangement. It does not replace the system winner and is not part of eligibility or final calibration proof.

**Why:** An operator may judge the curator Event or Compiled result publishable while still preferring a hand-arranged rescue. Coupling that preference to eligibility would let optional metadata grant or revoke Daily Drop access.

**How to apply:** Validate a preferred rescue against the current saved board when first recording the verdict, then preserve the receipt as history. Later feedback or missing rescue artifacts may affect how that preference is displayed, but never the curator approval, safety gates, freshness checks, or Daily Drop eligibility.

A separate exact-board path applies when the curator cannot form two complete boards: one saved nine-card arrangement made only from the current run's retained, displayable, non-excluded evidence may qualify after both human confirmations. The immutable receipt itself becomes the publication source; it does not approve future searches or manufacture an algorithmic winner.

**Why:** Visual editorial judgment can recognize a coherent narrative that brittle per-card metadata reports as 0/9. Publishing only the exact confirmed receipt fixes that false negative without granting blanket access to hidden, unavailable, excluded, changed, or future evidence.

**How to apply:** Keep ordinary preference metadata non-eligibility-bearing. For exact-board approval, verify the current run and contract, nine distinct candidates, receipt identity and board hash, both human confirmations, and fail closed if the stored receipt changes or disappears. The automatic Daily Drop must run fresh search, learned ranking, and curation first; an unused editorial board may serve only as a fallback.

The algorithm is a scout and first draft, not the editor-in-chief: semantic scoring may surface useful evidence while making an absurd narrative leap, so the human-approved board owns the final Vibe judgment.

**Why:** Automated relevance can produce entertaining but plainly wrong interpretations, such as treating a person-to-melon transformation as evidence for a character Vibe.

**How to apply:** Use algorithmic results to discover and filter retained evidence; never present an automatic score as the public meaning of a human-approved board, and keep an explicit editorial approval path for coherent boards the scorer misses.

Pairing publishability, exact-board approval, curator calibration, and completed learning are four separate states. Human-approved publishing must not wait for independent curator reproduction; keep that unfinished reproduction measurable in the private studio.

**Why:** Publishing is an editorial decision about an exact frozen edition, while calibration measures whether automation can reproduce that taste later. Coupling them lets an unfinished classifier hold a valid edition hostage.

**How to apply:** Label counters as automated recognition, never as objective Vibe truth. Permit one exact approved pairing to satisfy launch readiness; treat a second approved pairing as optional range and reserve inventory.

Approved rescue evidence becomes advisory calibration immediately, including broad visual choices inferred from selected-versus-omitted metadata. It may rerank fresh candidates, but it must not reorder or narrow search batches and must never satisfy a hard gate.

**Why:** Human taste such as “handsome man next to a building” is useful training evidence even when it is not semantic proof of a Vibe. Requiring reproduction before learning or eligibility made calibration too slow and gave the scorer authority over editorial judgment.

**How to apply:** Carry a bounded calibration profile into fresh Daily Drop curation; use definition cues, source/cluster contrasts, and pairwise choices only as a capped bonus after safety and promise evaluation. Keep transfer proof as private diagnostics, not an approval or eligibility requirement.

Calibration availability and Daily Drop publication are separate states. Only an exact rescue board explicitly approved as the publication source may be used as backup after fresh curation fails; ordinary rescue calibration and Collection export never grant that permission.

**Why:** Saved editorial work is useful training evidence, but treating every saved, exported, or preferred board as publishable would bypass the operator’s explicit final judgment. Repetition would also turn a backup into the default and erase daily freshness.

**How to apply:** Require the approved eligibility receipt to name that exact rescue board as its publication source. Record its provenance when used, compare exact board hashes against earlier Daily Drop manifests, and skip every previously published board.

When a pairing and Vibe recur, compare the proposed nine against prior manifests before publishing. If at least seven cards recur, refresh provider caches once; if the exact same nine remain, preserve the hero and rearrange the outer eight.

**Why:** A valid recurring pairing can still look stale when provider caches dominate the candidate pool. Reusing the same nine in the same composition makes a fresh search indistinguishable from replay.

**How to apply:** Trigger this only from prior publications of the same actor/Vibe pairing. Prefer a refreshed board when it reduces overlap; otherwise use a deterministic non-identical arrangement and record the intervention in curation provenance.
