---
name: Preflight preview publishing
description: Separates preflight approval, public teaser media publication, and dated Daily Drop editions.
---

**Rule:** A pairing need not have been Star of the Day to qualify for a public teaser. Its current preflight “yes, this is the vibe” and “yes, this is publishable” approval is the authority; the public teaser still requires exactly three safe MEDIA-backed cards from that approved board and substantive editorial context. Public reads must not disclose the approved nine-card board or trigger uncontrolled source-image downloads.

**Why:** Requiring a past daily edition incorrectly withheld reviewed non-daily alternatives and article-linked packs, while using actor definitions or article mentions alone would mistake a proposed pairing for an approved one.

**How to apply:** Revalidate the current preflight chain when materializing a teaser and again at public read time. Treat the teaser as its own bounded publication, not as a new Daily Drop or an approval of all future search results.

**Rule:** Treat an authenticated publication response as an attempted handoff, not proof that a teaser is live. Verify the public three-card projection separately, and keep the result beside the operator action where it remains visible on long audit pages.

**Why:** An operator could finish an approval or publication step without a publicly resolvable preview, while a notice displayed only at the top of the Lab left the outcome ambiguous. Mislabeling missing copy or a receipt conflict as absent approval sends operators through unnecessary editorial reviews.

**How to apply:** Give operators an independent, read-only live check for the exact pairing. Distinguish current-authority rejection, missing editorial copy, receipt conflicts, failed source copying, media registration, approval reads, and receipt storage with bounded categories, never upstream URLs or protected evidence. Do not label a preview published until its public read succeeds.

**Rule:** A source-health probe may test all nine images of either the current approved board or an operator's proposed rescue draft, but it is a point-in-time diagnostic, never media publication or editorial approval.

**Why:** An approved external thumbnail can expire at publication time. Testing only the first three cards misses a broken replacement elsewhere in the nine, while treating a reachable alternative as automatically publishable bypasses editorial review.

**How to apply:** Resolve image URLs only from retained current-run evidence on the server, never from a browser-supplied URL. Keep detailed probes private and return only bounded per-card health; after a rescue draft is approved, publish through the existing three-card MEDIA boundary and verify the public projection.

**Rule:** Validate guarded source transport before concluding that an entire approved board has unusable images. Transport compatibility fixes must preserve public-address validation.

**Why:** A shared transport failure can make every source appear unavailable even when the underlying images and editorial approval remain valid. Reworking the board does not repair that failure.

**How to apply:** Use a bounded, real fetch through the deployed guarded transport to distinguish source-health failures from transport failures before asking an operator to rearrange or re-review cards. Keep private-address rejection and the publication boundary intact; never relax them merely to make a preview publish.
